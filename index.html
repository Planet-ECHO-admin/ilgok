이 장학금이 담고 있는 것

일곡문화재단은 1995년에 설립한 공익재단입니다. 청소년과 청년이 어려운 상황에 끊임없이 도전하고, 긍정적인 태도를 기르며, 타인을 배려하고 인간관계를 소중히 여기는 미래 인재로 성장하도록 돕는 것이 재단의 뜻입니다.

올해 장학금은 창업 활동에서 성실성, 리더십, 소통 능력, 책임감, 봉사정신을 보여준 학생들의 다음 걸음을 응원하기 위해 마련되었습니다.

재단이 바라는 것은 단 하나입니다. 여러분 개인의 성취가 다시 친구와 학교, 지역으로 돌아가는 선순환입니다.

“생각의 틀을 깨다”

프로그램 전과 후, 세상과 문제를 바라보는 눈이 어떻게 달라졌나요?
  • 이 프로그램을 통해 내가 가장 달라진 점은 무엇인가요?
  • “이전에는 …라고 생각했지만, 이제는 …라고 생각한다.”라는 문장을 꼭 넣어 변화를 설명해 주세요.
  • 이번 프로그램이 가르쳐 준 인생의 교훈을 한 문장으로 정리한다면?
참고 예시 (그대로 옮기지 말고 흐름만 참고하세요)처음 아이디어를 낼 때 나는 ‘좋은 제품’이 곧 ‘팔리는 제품’이라고 믿었다. 그런데 반려동물 보호자 열 명을 인터뷰하고 나서 생각이 바뀌었다. 그들이 원한 것은 더 똑똑한 기계가 아니라 외출할 때 덜 불안한 마음이었다. 이전에는 기술이 문제를 해결한다고 생각했지만, 이제는 사람의 마음을 먼저 이해해야 기술이 쓸모를 갖는다고 생각한다.

“내 안의 잠재력을 깨우다”

실전 문제를 해결하며 새롭게 발견한 나의 강점은 무엇인가요?
  • 문제를 해결하면서 가장 중요하다고 느낀 점은 무엇이었나요?
  • 스스로 발견한 ‘내 안의 새로운 능력’은 무엇인가요?
  • 앞으로 이 능력을 활용해 어떤 도전이나 변화를 시도해 보고 싶나요?
참고 예시발표 전날, 우리 팀의 수익 구조가 맞지 않는다는 것을 발견했다. 모두 지쳐 있었지만 나는 엑셀을 열고 숫자를 하나씩 다시 맞췄다. 그때 알았다. 나는 앞에 나서서 말하는 사람은 아니지만, 모두가 놓친 빈틈을 끝까지 붙잡는 사람이라는 것을.

“미래의 지도를 그리다”

창업 경험이 여러분의 직업관과 꿈에 어떤 영향을 주었나요?
  • 창업을 통해 배운 진로와 직업의 의미는 무엇인가요?
  • 이 경험으로 새롭게 생긴 꿈이나 구체적인 목표가 있나요?
  • 이번 경험이 준 가르침을 한 문장으로 요약해 주세요.
참고 예시나는 막연히 ‘개발자가 되고 싶다’고만 말해 왔다. 앱을 기획하며 고객 인터뷰, 가격 정하기, 발표까지 해 보니 코드를 짜는 일은 전체 과정의 한 조각이었다. 이제 나의 목표는 조금 더 구체적이다. 사람들이 실제로 겪는 불편을 먼저 찾아내고, 그 해결책을 직접 만들 수 있는 사람이 되는 것이다.

What this scholarship is about

The ILGOK Foundation is a nonprofit established in 1995. Its mission is to help young people grow into future leaders who embrace challenges, stay positive, care about others, and value the relationships in their lives.

This year’s scholarship supports the next step for students who showed diligence, leadership, strong communication, responsibility, and a spirit of service through their entrepreneurship projects.

The Foundation hopes for just one thing: that your success comes full circle and gives back to your friends, your school, and your community.

How we hope you’ll use it

We encourage you to put the scholarship toward intellectual property (IP) projects, research and development (R&D), or international exchange — camps, competitions, exhibitions, and visits to schools or organizations abroad. That said, you’re free to use it however you think will help you most.

Essay guide

This section is for middle and high school recipients. Your reflection essay is an important record of the program and of how you’ve grown. It’s also a gift to yourself. The weeks you spent hashing out ideas with your teammates will fade faster than you think. Writing about them now gives you something to come back to years from now, when you’re figuring out your next step.

Requirements

ItemRequirement (same for everyone)
Length1,000–1,500 Korean characters (about one A4 page)
FormatAny format; choose one of the four themes
View a sample essay

Pick one of four themes

“Thinking outside the box”

How has the way you see the world and its problems changed since the program began?
  • What’s the biggest way you’ve changed through this program?
  • Describe that change using the sentence “I used to think …, but now I think ….”
  • If you had to sum up the biggest lesson from this program in one sentence, what would it be?
Example (borrow the flow, not the words)When we first pitched our idea, I assumed a good product was the same thing as a product that sells. That changed after we interviewed ten pet owners. What they wanted wasn’t a smarter gadget — it was peace of mind when they left the house. I used to think technology solves problems, but now I think technology only becomes useful once you truly understand people.

“Unlocking your potential”

What new strength did you discover in yourself while tackling real problems?
  • What did you find mattered most when solving problems?
  • What new ability did you discover in yourself?
  • What challenge or change would you like to take on with it?
ExampleThe night before our pitch, I realized our revenue model didn’t add up. Everyone was exhausted, but I opened the spreadsheet and reworked the numbers one by one. That’s when it hit me: I’m not the person who does the talking up front, but I am the person who catches what everyone else missed — and doesn’t let it go.

“Mapping out the future”

How did this startup experience shape the way you think about work and your dreams?
  • What did it teach you about careers and what work means?
  • Did it spark a new dream or give you a concrete goal?
  • Sum up what this experience taught you in one sentence.
ExampleFor years, all I’d say was that I wanted to be a developer. But building an app meant interviewing customers, setting prices, and pitching — and I realized coding was just one piece of the puzzle. Now my goal is sharper: to be someone who spots the real frustrations people deal with and builds the fix myself.

“A letter to myself, five years from now”

Building on what you’ve accomplished, picture the person you’ll be.
  • What would you want your future self to remember about this experience?
  • Include the lessons you learned and the new goals you’ve set.
  • Describe the kind of person you want to become and the changes you want to make.
ExampleDear future me, you’re probably swamped with college or your first job right now. At sixteen, I learned for the first time just how much the words “our team” can carry. Don’t forget the night I finished our presentation with a friend I hadn’t spoken to in three days after we’d argued.

How to structure a strong essay

  1. Open with a sceneStart with a specific moment — “At our very first team meeting…” — to draw the reader in.
  2. Show the struggleWrite about what was hard and what you tried. Attempts that failed make great material, too.
  3. Reach the turning pointExplain what shifted in your thinking or in yourself along the way.
  4. Look aheadClose with how you plan to use the scholarship and who you want to become.

Before you submit

  • Does your essay read as one connected story rather than a list of answers?
  • Does it include details only you could write — your team, your product, your customers?
  • Is it entirely your own work, not copied from someone else or written by AI?
  • Is there anything you’d like to say to the Foundation or the people you worked with?

Privacy and important notes

Your personal information will be used only to disburse the scholarship, verify recipients, and prepare supporting records. It will be securely deleted in accordance with applicable regulations once it’s no longer needed.

  • Make sure your account number and account holder match your bankbook exactly. Even a single typo can delay your scholarship.
  • If the scholarship is going to a parent’s account, the account holder must be the parent named in the form.
  • ID numbers are used only to process your scholarship and are deleted afterward.
  • Excerpts from essays may be shared anonymously in the Foundation’s reports and promotional materials. You can opt out in the form.
  • If you made a mistake on the form, please don’t submit it again — contact Minjung Kim instead.

FAQ

I don’t have a bank account in my name.

Middle and high school students can receive the scholarship through a parent’s account. Just select “parent’s account” in the form and enter your parent’s ID number and account details.

I’m an international student and don’t have a Korean bank account.

Please contact us before submitting the form, and we’ll walk you through how the scholarship can be disbursed.

Is the process the same for out-of-school youth?

Yes. Just enter “학교 밖 청소년” (out-of-school youth) in place of a school name.

What format should my essay be in?

Any format works. Aim for 1,000–1,500 characters and send the finished file by email or KakaoTalk. The sample essay is a good place to start.

Do I have to spend the scholarship on something specific?

We encourage IP projects, R&D, and international exchange, but you’re free to decide what works best for you. If you can, send us a quick note later about how you used it — it means a lot to the Foundation.
